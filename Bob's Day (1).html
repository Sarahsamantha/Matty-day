Basics

"Auto" is lively, then goes calm when a check-in says mood is up.
A countdown pops up 5 minutes before each block ends, showing what's next. Works while the app is open on screen.
Later blocks move to make room. Work and your sleep routine (wake, meds, wind down, bed) never move.
Turns off the extra helpers in one go: lunch and evening prompts, reality check, missed-block card, premenstrual card, money card, and auto-booked jobs and self-care. Your schedule, ticks and data stay exactly as they are. Switch back any time.
Auto-booked items show “✨ picked for you”. Tap one to swap or remove it.
Hide them if they start to feel like pressure. Your progress charts still show. Celebrations also quieten by themselves when mood is high or sleep has been short.
Daily mood rating helps some people and wears on others. If it starts to feel like a chore or a reminder of being unwell, switch it off. Sleep and rhythm still give early warnings.

Why it's designed this way

One thing at a time. The home screen shows only now, next, and the rhythm anchors. Less on screen means less load on working memory.

Time you can see. The ring empties as the block runs down and turns amber 5 minutes before the switch, as a transition warning. The strip shows the whole day.

Park it, don't chase it. When a thought pops up mid-task, write it down and come back later. Adult ADHD CBT calls this "distractibility delay".

The day bends to the brain. The check-in suggests a Full, Steady or Minimum day. Doing your musts on a hard day earns a milestone too.

Growth means rhythm. The headline number is how regular your days are, because regular routines protect mood in bipolar disorder (IPSRT). It isn't about how much you got done.

A satisfying end, then rest. Close the day with a short recap, then the app goes quiet. After bedtime there's nothing to scroll.

Mood is optional. Research shows daily mood rating helps some people and worsens things for others, so it can be switched off.
